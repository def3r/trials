Quantum-kernel reliability · c2q_tsp (QAOA)

TSP's real ceiling isn't the 28-qubit simulator limit — it's four cities, and this hardware couldn't even finish a fifth.

Every other kernel in this project encodes one qubit per node. TSP encodes one qubit per (city, position) pair — N² instead of N — so the 28-qubit ceiling arrives at just 6 cities, and completeness has already collapsed to zero one city before that. This is the shortest usable range of any kernel evaluated in this project, by a wide margin.

Complete collapse by N=4

4/10 correct at N=3 (9 qubits) — already the worst cheapest-tier result of any kernel tested. 0/10 correct at N=4 (16 qubits), with all 10 trials returning an invalid tour. N=5 (25 qubits) was attempted and aborted after a single seed ran past 20 minutes without completing, sustaining 100% GPU utilization at 78°C the whole time — not a hang, just genuinely too expensive to finish on this hardware.

01

Squaring the qubit count changes everything downstream

Every other graph kernel in this project one-hot encodes a single property per node — MaxCut and clique use 1 qubit/node, k-coloring uses k qubits/node. TSP encodes which position in the tour each city occupies, which needs a full N×N one-hot grid: N² qubits. At the same node count, TSP is already the most expensive kernel in the project by a wide margin — and it crosses the 28-qubit simulator ceiling at just 6 nodes, confirmed directly:

02

No partial credit: every trial was either exactly right or not a tour at all

Complete graph, symmetric integer weights, ground truth from classical brute force. decode_tsp only returns a real distance for a bitstring that decodes to a genuine permutation visiting every city exactly once — anything else comes back as -1 ("invalid"), the same sound-when-it-answers property kcolor and clique's decoders have. What's notable here: in this sample, there was no middle ground — no trial returned a valid-but-suboptimal tour. Every one of the 20 trials across N=3 and N=4 was either exactly optimal or not a valid tour at all.

This isn't a claim that a valid-but-suboptimal tour is structurally impossible — decode_tsp would return its actual distance if one came back. It's an empirical observation about this QUBO landscape: the permutation constraint (one-hot per city and per position, N² variables with a dense penalty structure between them) appears to be nearly all-or-nothing for QAOA at layers=2 — it either lands exactly on a valid permutation or misses the constraint manifold entirely. Compare to clique/kcolor, where a wrong answer is usually still a close wrong answer.

03

A real qubo_tsp bug, found and fixed — it wasn't the cause

The severity of the collapse above prompted a direct check on whether qubo_tsp itself was even constructing the right optimization target — pure classical computation, no GPU: brute-force the QUBO's own true minimum over every possible bitstring and compare it to the classical optimum.

Found two real bugs. (1) The distance-cost term never scored the tour's closing edge (position N−1 back to position 0), even though the scoring function (decode_tsp) does close the loop. (2) Q is upper-triangular by convention, but roughly half of the distance-cost writes landed in the lower triangle — a cell nothing downstream ever reads — silently discarding about half the intended cost signal. Confirmed directly: at N=4, the old QUBO's own true minimum (found by brute force, not QAOA) was a valid tour of length 48; the actual optimum is 35. Both bugs are now fixed in c2cudaq/include/c2cudaq/internal.h, and re-verified: the corrected QUBO's minimum now exactly matches the classical optimum at both N=3 and N=4.

3 (9 qubits)4/10 correct, 6/10 invalid1/10 correct, 9/10 invalid

Re-running the actual QAOA stress test after the fix found no improvement — slightly worse on this small sample. This is the useful negative result: the bug was real and worth fixing (a genuine correctness defect in the optimization target itself, independent of QAOA), but it isn't what's causing the empirical collapse. The constraint-satisfaction terms were already correct and untouched by either bug. What remains is a genuine QAOA convergence difficulty on a now provably-correct problem — which makes the case for keeping tsp_impl classical-only stronger, not weaker: this isn't a bug waiting to be found.

Contrast with clique/kcolor, where multi-minute single-seed spot checks at least completed in the 1–4 minute range near their own ceilings (see analysis/clique/clique.md) — TSP's N=5 didn't reach that within 20× that budget.

Of the four QAOA-based kernels evaluated across this project (MaxCut, KColor, Clique, TSP), this is the clearest "not there yet" result — and, per §03, provably not for lack of trying to find (and fix) a bug. It isn't unsound — a returned non-negative objective really was a genuine, exactly-optimal tour in every trial observed — but the N² qubit cost compounds with a genuine QAOA convergence difficulty on the permutation constraint, confirmed to persist even against a QUBO landscape now proven correct. Together those factors make the kernel's *usable* range (N≤3, marginally) narrower than any other kernel's tested ceiling by a wide margin. This matches bridge.cpp's existing decision to leave tsp_impl classical-only — this stress test is a confirmation of that choice, not a reason to revisit it.
